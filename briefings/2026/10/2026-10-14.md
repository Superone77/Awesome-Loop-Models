# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-14

## Outcome

Fourteen papers were added. Each reapplies a shared learned block or processor within one
forward process. Two concern how looped transformers are trained and extrapolated in
depth, three study released looped language models (safety at reduced depth, latent
monitoring, quantization), five improve or analyse TRM-style recursive reasoners, and four
apply looped models to diffusion, tabular data, vehicle routing and speech. Four of the
additions cite Benhao Huang's Equilibrium Reasoners (EqR):
[Looped Reasoning Finishes Earlier Than You Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18174) and
[Input-Conditioned Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18547) use EqR as a baseline,
[Tiny Recursive Message Passing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18649) uses EqR's Maze-Unique
data, and [Resample the Answer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16427) discusses it in related
work.

## Added

### Adaptive-Depth Is a Finishing Stage

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14252) asks when looped transformers need to see
many loop depths during training. Its models have two untied prelude blocks, an
eight-block recurrent group applied K times, and two untied coda blocks. Fixed-first
training holds depth fixed for the first 67 or 80 percent of updates and then samples K
uniformly from 1 to 16. At 200M parameters, depth 2 for 67 percent of training cuts
modeled compute by 45.3 percent and keeps mean NLL across serving depths within 0.5
percent of uniform training; with K=4 for 67 percent, mean NLL is 0.68643 against 0.68582
at 31.3 percent less compute, while a compute-matched uniform run reaches only 0.72089. At
1B parameters the two schedules cut wall-clock time by 32.1 and 38.5 percent, with
terminal NLL within 0.8 percent and downstream scores within seed variation.

Why it was added: it is a training-time depth schedule for a shared recurrent block.

### Learning to Extrapolate in Depth with Looped Transformers

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14456) studies how far a looped block trained at
one depth can be unrolled at test time, using all-pairs graph connectivity. A single
row-normalized linear-attention block is shared across loops and trained on the terminal
loss. Under exchangeable training data and symmetric positive initialization, population
gradient flow reduces to one effective parameter, which balances propagation against
fitting unresolved structure. This creates a finite window of useful test depths: too few
loops under-propagate and too many create false connections. The paper certifies that one
test depth solves harder graphs, with a gain that grows exponentially as training depth
outpaces training hardness, and confirms on Erdős–Rényi and path graphs that the windows
are conservative. The analysis assumes linear attention and population training.

Why it was added: it is theory of depth extrapolation for a weight-tied looped block.

### SpikCTU: A Compact Spiking Network with Adaptive Recurrent Computation for Speech Command Recognition

[SpikCTU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14594) separates input encoding from recurrent depth
in a spiking speech-command model. A hybrid spiking embedding builds a fixed memory, and
one shared Continuous Thinking Unit then repeatedly reads it with attention and updates a
binary spike state; only low-rank stage adapters change every two steps. Each step adds a
correction to a base prediction, which supports confidence-based early exit. With 0.16 to
0.17M parameters it reaches 96.60 percent on SHD, 81.74 on SSC and 95.96 on GSC. Early exit
cuts recurrent evaluations by 59 percent on SSC and 67 percent on GSC. The best depth is
task-dependent: going past the optimum costs 4.15 points on SHD. Code:
[Rivflyyy/spikctu](https://github.com/Rivflyyy/spikctu).

Why it was added: a shared module is iterated in depth, separately from the time steps of
the input.

### Fitting Every Hint Is Not Enough: Premature Floods in Recurrent Algorithmic Reasoners

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14982) studies a failure of recurrent neural
algorithmic reasoners trained with per-step hints on small graphs and run for many more
steps on large ones. On BFS in SALSA-CLRS, a GRU message-passing processor of about 200,000
parameters, applied once per algorithm step, can reproduce every training hint and still
"flood": it marks nodes reached before the search arrives. Under full BPTT 6 of 23 seeds
flood, and detaching the hidden state between steps gives 0 of 23. The flood is carried
in the hidden state and triggered by degree inputs in the tail of the training
distribution; clamping that input or adding 200 dense training graphs removes it.
Detachment does not protect neighbor-sum or residual-update processors, and the
benchmark's GIN floods from step 18 on its 1,600-node test set in one seed.

Why it was added: it is a failure-mode analysis of a shared processor iterated beyond its
training depth.

### Recurrent In-Context Learning for Tabular Foundation Models

[Tab-Loop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14998) replaces the 12 untied in-context-learning
blocks of TabICLv2 with 4 tied blocks applied k times, re-injecting the encoded input
before each application and keeping encoders, decoder, prior and recipe unchanged. At
matched pretraining compute and 12 block applications it matches the untied baseline
with 62 percent fewer parameters (10.4M vs 27.6M): held-out log loss 0.4583 vs 0.4588,
TALENT accuracy 84.10 vs 83.92 percent, TabArena Elo 1409 vs 1396. A parameter-matched
4-block untied control trails both. Without retraining, Elo rises from 1288 at one loop
to 1424 at four, and a second stage with longer sampled loops reaches about 1548 on
TabArena-Lite at 24 to 32 loops. Results are from one seed, and the deeper settings use
more inference compute than the baseline.

Why it was added: it is a looped in-context-learning stack with iso-parameter and
iso-compute comparisons against untied layers.

### RELAGRAM: Recursive Latent Graph Model for the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem

[RELAGRAM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15106) brings TRM-style latent recursion to the
capacitated vehicle routing problem. A shared two-block transformer repeatedly updates a
workspace state and an answer state over edge tokens, and a masked decoder turns the
resulting edge heatmap into routes. Trained only on CVRP-100 with REINFORCE and
self-imitation, the 0.53M-parameter model reaches gaps to PyVRP of 13.40 percent greedy and
8.30 percent with 256 candidates, and transfers zero-shot to CVRP-500 (15.64) and CVRP-1000
(21.65), where extra depth improves the greedy gap from 29.91 to 28.45 percent. DeepACO is
stronger in eight of nine regimes, and a matched single-pass control shows no
statistically significant gain from recursion (18.89 vs 18.47 percent).

Why it was added: it applies a recursive shared block to graph combinatorial optimization
and reports a matched non-recursive control.

### Running Fewer Loops Removes Refusal Behaviour in Recurrent-Depth Language Models

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15218) studies four released Ouro checkpoints and
Huginn-0125 when served with fewer loops than trained. At half depth, Ouro-1.4B-Thinking
keeps 89 percent of its GSM8K accuracy (0.692 vs 0.775), while harmful compliance rises
from 0.038 to 0.205; Ouro-2.6B-Thinking at one loop goes from 0.035 to 0.344. Probes still
detect harmfulness after one loop, but the refusal direction has not settled, and adding
it back at one loop restores refusal. A dense Qwen3-1.7B with layers removed loses task
ability first instead. The models' own exit gates do not help. A random-loop LoRA safety
objective raises the one-loop refusal gap to +0.399 (vs +0.118 for final-loop training)
and keeps Huginn's harmful compliance at or below 0.040 across 1 to 32 loops.

Why it was added: it is a depth-dependent safety failure of shared-block recurrence, with
a fix trained across loop counts.

### LoopQuant: Accurate and Efficient Post-Training Quantization for Looped Transformers

[LoopQuant](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16368) targets weight-activation quantization of
looped language models, where one shared module sees a different activation distribution
at each loop and early errors propagate. It learns one orthogonal-scaling transform and
channel reordering shared by all loops, assigns 4- or 8-bit activations per loop from
Shapley-style sensitivities under a bit budget, and fits per-loop clipping, gain and bias.
On Ouro-2.6B with 4-bit activations in half the loops, it reaches 70.20 percent average
zero-shot accuracy, 1.90 points above the strongest baseline, and lowers WikiText2
perplexity from 9.09 to 8.04. At W4A4 it beats W4A8 baselines while cutting memory by 65.3
percent and decoding 1.7x faster than BF16. Code:
[Kishon-zzx/LoopQuant](https://github.com/Kishon-zzx/LoopQuant).

Why it was added: it is a quantization scheme designed around the iterations of a shared
block.

### Resample the Answer: Where to Spend Test-Time Compute in Recursive Reasoning

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16427) asks which latent state of the Tiny
Recursive Model carries a failure. Re-opening only puzzles whose halting logit is
negative, it compares continuing or restarting failed runs with noise on the reasoning
state, as in PTRM, against resampling the answer state. Resampling the answer state
recovers more failures than any reasoning-state perturbation at equal compute, and the gap
widens with budget; restarting works as well as continuing, so retries can run in
parallel. Gated Answer Resampling, with at most 64 restarts and no training, takes TRM
from 85.9 to 99.4 percent on Sudoku-Extreme and from 68.8 to 85.6 percent on Pencil Puzzle
Bench validation, beating PTRM on the same checkpoints even when PTRM uses 42 to 197 times
as much compute. It is untested beyond TRM.

Why it was added: it analyses where failures live in a recursive reasoner's two latent
states.

**Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, ICML 2026) is discussed in the
introduction and related work as training with noise on both states, including its
initial-state ablation; it is not a baseline.

### LoopREG: Representation Supervision Enables Test-Time Recurrent Depth in Diffusion Transformers

[LoopREG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17877) adds shared-block recurrence inside each
denoiser call of the REG diffusion Transformer. The block whose output is aligned to frozen
DINOv2 features is re-applied, with alignment after the final training-time visit, and
gets extra visits at inference with no new parameters. On ImageNet 256x256 at 400K steps,
SiT-B/2 FID falls from 15.08 for the local REG baseline to 4.91. In a model trained with
two visits, a third visit lowers FID from 14.71 to 5.34 for one extra block execution.
Gains persist on L/2 (4.23 to 3.21) and XL/2 (3.38 to 2.84). Plain looped SiT degrades
with an extra visit (34.45 to 46.41), and the gains come with lower recall.

Why it was added: the loop is a shared block inside one denoiser forward pass; diffusion
is only the outer sampler.

### Monitoring latent reasoning in looped language models

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18127) applies five interpretability lenses
(state settling, commitment iteration, per-iteration readout, attention-head roles, noise
injection) to eight released looped LMs from four families. It separates carry loops
(Ouro, LoopUS), where the prompt enters only through the looped state, from re-read loops
(Huginn, Rec-OLMo, Parcae), which re-inject the prompt each iteration. In six of eight
models, 76 to 89 percent of generated tokens already settle on their final top-1 token at
the first iteration. Ouro-Thinking models are best at iteration 1 and get worse with
further loops, and only Huginn improves steadily. Noise at four times the entry-state norm
breaks every carry model, while re-read models tolerate fifty times; at a smaller amplitude
Ouro-1.4B's refusal rate drops from 73 to 25 percent.

Why it was added: it is a mechanistic and monitoring study of shared-block latent
iteration.

### Looped Reasoning Finishes Earlier Than You Think

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18174) analyses why recurrent reasoners such as
FPRM and HRM settle slowly near solution roots. A Sylvester decomposition of the local
Jacobian shows that a slowly decaying oscillatory mode imposes its rate on the coupled
latent dynamics, which causal interventions confirm. QRR, a gauge-equivariant looped block
with momentum and halting on a quotient-space fixed-point residual, reaches 97.9 percent on
Sudoku-Extreme, 85.8 on Maze-Hard, 97.0 on Maze-Unique and 62.4 Pass@1 on Mini-ARC with 7M
parameters, against 83.7, 85.3, 94.7 and 57.1 for reproduced FPRM. On Sudoku-Extreme the
median halt step falls from 122 to 27 and the false-halt rate from 8.9 to 0.1 percent,
although each step costs more (34.7 vs 22.3 ms). Code:
[camail-official/Quotient_Recurrent_Reasoner_QRR](https://github.com/camail-official/Quotient_Recurrent_Reasoner_QRR).

Why it was added: it explains and fixes slow convergence of a shared recurrent block.

**Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, 2026) is a baseline in Table 1
(89.9 on Sudoku-Extreme, 88.9 on Maze-Unique), the Maze-Unique benchmark follows the EqR
construction, and EqR is discussed in related work.

### Input-Conditioned Search for Efficient Test-Time Scaling of Recursive Reasoners

[ICS](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18547) adds a test-time scaling axis for frozen Tiny
Recursive Models: instead of more updates or perturbed latents, it changes the
input-conditioned update map by injecting tentative partial assignments or applying
validity-preserving restatements, and reruns the reasoner from a fresh state. The paper
argues that depth and noise-based scaling can stay trapped in incorrect attractors, and
uses computer-assisted proofs on trained Sudoku reasoners to show that changing the input
embedding can remove an incorrect attracting fixed point through a fold bifurcation. On
Sudoku-Extreme, TRM with depth scaling scores 87.85 percent at 0.38 TFLOPs, while ICS
scores 99.81 percent at 1.12 TFLOPs. Across six tasks it reports the highest mean accuracy
with up to two orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs than the scaling baselines. Code:
[icsearch/ICS](https://github.com/icsearch/ICS).

Why it was added: it is test-time scaling and fixed-point analysis for a recursive
reasoner. It is borderline because each search branch is a full rerun of the reasoner;
the catalog already files comparable TRM inference wrappers such as PTRM.

**Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, ICML 2026) is a baseline in the
main comparison, run from the official implementation, and is cited for Maze-Unique being
near-saturated.

### Tiny Recursive Message Passing

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18649) carries the training and inference recipe
of tiny recursive transformers (deep supervision, truncated recursion, ACT halting,
deduction lattices) over to typed recurrent message-passing networks. Weight-shared GRU
and Deep Sets modules run inner rounds while an answer state is held fixed, and a small GRU
updates the answer each outer cycle. A 16k-parameter model reaches about 99 percent on
Sudoku-Extreme and trains in about 10 minutes on an H100; best-of-64 noisy passes reach
99.9 percent on Sudoku-Extreme and 100 percent on Maze-Unique. The cost of truncating
gradients grows with how many rounds information needs to cross the graph, so wiring
trades robustness to truncation against transfer across sizes. The models transfer
zero-shot to larger N-Queens boards and harder SAT formulas, matching or beating NeuroSAT
and QuerySAT with a fraction of their parameters.

Why it was added: it trains a shared message-passing processor as a deep recursive
reasoner.

**Cites Benhao's work:** yes. It uses the maze-30x30-unique set released with EqR
(locuslab/EqR) and cites EqR in related work among noise-injection inference methods.

## Near misses

- [Coarse-Graining Recursive Expert Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14413)
  replaces spans of untied GPT-2 and Qwen3 blocks with cheaper macros; its only shared
  recursion is a small toy prototype.
- [DeepThink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16171) proposes a shared thinking operator over a
  knowledge bank, but it is purely conceptual, has no experiments, and parameter sharing is
  optional.
- [FON](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16301) uses content-adaptive recursion across sequence
  positions; its optional depth reuse is an ablation, not the contribution.
- [DEFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16597) solves an implicit Euler flow-policy sampler
  as an equilibrium system, which is flow-based iterative inference rather than a reused
  representation block.
- [Dynamic K-Head](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17679) selects the number of CoDI continuous
  thought steps, which feed the last hidden state back through the full model as the next
  input.
- [LoOPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18162) runs a searched block of pretrained layers
  twice to build a self-distillation teacher, but the deployed student does not loop.
