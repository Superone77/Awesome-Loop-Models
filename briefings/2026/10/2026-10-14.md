# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-14

## Outcome

Fourteen papers were added. Each reapplies a shared learned block or processor within one
forward process. Two concern how looped transformers are trained and extrapolated in
depth, three study released looped language models (safety at reduced depth, latent
monitoring, quantization), five improve or analyse TRM-style recursive reasoners, and four
apply looped models to diffusion, tabular data, vehicle routing and speech. Four of the
additions cite Benhao Huang's Equilibrium Reasoners (EqR):
[Looped Reasoning Finishes Earlier Than You Think](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18174) and
[Input-Conditioned Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18547) use EqR as a baseline,
[Tiny Recursive Message Passing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18649) uses EqR's Maze-Unique
data, and [Resample the Answer](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16427) discusses it in related
work.

## Added

### Adaptive-Depth Is a Finishing Stage

[arXiv:2610.14252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14252)

**TL;DR:** Train a looped transformer at one fixed depth for most of the run and sample loop depths only at the end; this saves 31–45 percent of training compute at about the same NLL across serving depths.

- **Setup:** prelude-core-coda LMs (two untied prelude blocks, an eight-block shared group looped K times, two untied coda blocks) at 200M and 1B parameters.
- **Method:** fixed-first training holds K fixed for the first 67 or 80 percent of updates, then samples K uniformly from 1 to 16.
- **Results (200M):** K=2 for 67 percent cuts modeled compute by 45.3 percent with mean serving-depth NLL within 0.5 percent of uniform training; K=4 gives 0.68643 vs 0.68582 at 31.3 percent less compute, while a compute-matched uniform run reaches only 0.72089.
- **Results (1B):** wall-clock time falls by 32.1 and 38.5 percent, with terminal NLL within 0.8 percent and downstream scores within seed variation.
- **Caveat:** no comparison against untied models.
- **Why added:** a training-time depth schedule for a shared recurrent block.

### Learning to Extrapolate in Depth with Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2610.14456](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14456)

**TL;DR:** Theory showing that a looped attention block trained at one depth has a finite window of test-time loop counts that solve harder graphs, with a certified extrapolation gain.

- **Setup:** all-pairs graph connectivity; one row-normalized linear-attention block shared across loops, trained on the terminal loss only.
- **Theory:** under exchangeable data and symmetric positive initialization, population gradient flow reduces to one effective parameter that converges to an explicit optimum.
- **Finding:** that optimum gives a finite window of useful test depths: too few loops under-propagate, too many create false connections.
- **Extrapolation:** one test depth certifiably solves harder graphs, with a gain that grows exponentially as training depth outpaces training hardness; Erdős–Rényi and path-graph experiments confirm the windows are conservative.
- **Caveat:** linear attention, population training and symmetric initialization.
- **Why added:** theory of depth extrapolation for a weight-tied looped block.

### SpikCTU: A Compact Spiking Network with Adaptive Recurrent Computation for Speech Command Recognition

[arXiv:2610.14594](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14594) · Code: [Rivflyyy/spikctu](https://github.com/Rivflyyy/spikctu)

**TL;DR:** A 0.16M-parameter spiking speech-command model that iterates one shared "thinking unit" over a fixed memory and exits early when confident.

- **Mechanism:** a hybrid spiking embedding builds a fixed memory; one shared Continuous Thinking Unit repeatedly reads it with attention and updates a binary spike state; only low-rank stage adapters change every two steps.
- **Depth control:** each step adds a correction to a base prediction, enabling confidence-based early exit.
- **Results:** 96.60 percent on SHD, 81.74 on SSC and 95.96 on GSC with 0.16–0.17M parameters; early exit cuts recurrent evaluations by 59 percent (SSC) and 67 percent (GSC).
- **Caveat:** the best depth is task-dependent; going past it costs 4.15 points on SHD.
- **Why added:** a shared module iterated in depth, separately from the input's time steps.

### Fitting Every Hint Is Not Enough: Premature Floods in Recurrent Algorithmic Reasoners

[arXiv:2610.14982](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14982)

**TL;DR:** A recurrent algorithmic reasoner can reproduce every BFS training hint and still fail on deep graphs by "flooding" early; the cause is drift in the carried hidden state.

- **Setup:** BFS in SALSA-CLRS with a ~200,000-parameter GRU message-passing processor applied once per algorithm step, trained on small graphs and run for many more steps on large ones.
- **Failure:** the processor marks nodes reached before the search arrives; 6 of 23 BPTT seeds flood.
- **Cause:** the flood lives in the hidden state and is triggered by degree inputs in the tail of the training distribution.
- **Fixes:** detaching the state between steps gives 0 of 23; clamping the input or adding 200 dense training graphs also removes it.
- **Caveat:** detachment does not protect neighbor-sum or residual processors; the benchmark's own GIN floods from step 18 on its 1,600-node test set in one seed; BFS only.
- **Why added:** failure-mode analysis of a shared processor iterated past its training depth.

### Recurrent In-Context Learning for Tabular Foundation Models

[arXiv:2610.14998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14998)

**TL;DR:** Tab-Loop swaps TabICLv2's 12 untied in-context-learning blocks for 4 tied blocks looped three times and matches it with 62 percent fewer parameters.

- **Mechanism:** 4 tied blocks applied k times, re-injecting the encoded input before each application; encoders, decoder, prior and recipe unchanged.
- **Matched comparison (12 applications, equal compute):** 10.4M vs 27.6M parameters; log loss 0.4583 vs 0.4588; TALENT 84.10 vs 83.92 percent; TabArena Elo 1409 vs 1396. A parameter-matched 4-block untied control trails both.
- **Test-time depth:** without retraining, Elo rises from 1288 at one loop to 1424 at four; a second stage with longer sampled loops reaches about 1548 on TabArena-Lite at 24–32 loops.
- **Caveat:** one seed; the deeper settings use more inference compute than the baseline.
- **Why added:** a looped in-context-learning stack with iso-parameter and iso-compute comparisons against untied layers.

### RELAGRAM: Recursive Latent Graph Model for the Capacitated Vehicle Routing Problem

[arXiv:2610.15106](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15106)

**TL;DR:** TRM-style latent recursion for vehicle routing; it transfers to 10x larger instances, but a matched single-pass control is just as good.

- **Mechanism:** a shared two-block transformer repeatedly updates workspace and answer states over edge tokens; a masked decoder turns the edge heatmap into routes. Trained with REINFORCE plus self-imitation.
- **Results:** 0.53M parameters, trained on CVRP-100; gap to PyVRP 13.40 percent greedy, 8.30 percent with 256 candidates; zero-shot 15.64 (CVRP-500) and 21.65 (CVRP-1000).
- **Test-time depth:** on CVRP-1000, extra depth improves the greedy gap from 29.91 to 28.45 percent.
- **Caveat:** a matched single-pass control shows no significant gain from recursion (18.89 vs 18.47 percent); DeepACO is stronger in eight of nine regimes.
- **Why added:** a recursive shared block for graph combinatorial optimization, with a matched non-recursive control.

### Running Fewer Loops Removes Refusal Behaviour in Recurrent-Depth Language Models

[arXiv:2610.15218](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15218)

**TL;DR:** Serving Ouro or Huginn with fewer loops keeps most task ability but strips refusal; training safety at random loop depths fixes it.

- **Setup:** four released Ouro checkpoints and Huginn-0125 run below their trained depth.
- **Finding:** at half depth, Ouro-1.4B-Thinking keeps 89 percent of GSM8K accuracy (0.692 vs 0.775) while harmful compliance rises from 0.038 to 0.205; Ouro-2.6B-Thinking at one loop goes from 0.035 to 0.344.
- **Mechanism:** probes still detect harmfulness after one loop, but the refusal direction has not settled; adding it back at one loop restores refusal. A dense Qwen3-1.7B with layers removed loses task ability first instead.
- **Fix:** the models' own exit gates do not help; a random-loop LoRA safety objective raises the one-loop refusal gap to +0.399 (vs +0.118 for final-loop training) and keeps Huginn at or below 0.040 harmful compliance across 1–32 loops.
- **Why added:** a depth-dependent safety failure of shared-block recurrence, fixed by training across loop counts.

### LoopQuant: Accurate and Efficient Post-Training Quantization for Looped Transformers

[arXiv:2610.16368](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16368) · Code: [Kishon-zzx/LoopQuant](https://github.com/Kishon-zzx/LoopQuant)

**TL;DR:** Post-training quantization designed for looped LMs; at W4A4 on Ouro-2.6B it beats W4A8 baselines with 65 percent less memory.

- **Problem:** one shared module sees a different activation distribution at each loop, and early quantization error propagates.
- **Method:** one orthogonal-scaling transform and channel reordering shared by all loops; 4- or 8-bit activations per loop chosen from Shapley-style sensitivities under a bit budget; per-loop clipping, gain and bias.
- **Results (Ouro-2.6B):** with 4-bit activations in half the loops, 70.20 percent average zero-shot accuracy (+1.90 over the best baseline) and WikiText2 perplexity 9.09 → 8.04; at W4A4, 65.3 percent less memory and 1.7x faster decoding than BF16.
- **Caveat:** the LoopQ baseline is compared only through its reported numbers.
- **Why added:** a quantization scheme built around the iterations of a shared block.

### Resample the Answer: Where to Spend Test-Time Compute in Recursive Reasoning

[arXiv:2610.16427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16427)

**TL;DR:** TRM failures live in the answer state, not the reasoning state; resampling only the answer state of rejected runs lifts Sudoku-Extreme from 85.9 to 99.4 percent.

- **Setup:** re-open only puzzles whose halting logit is negative; compare continuing or restarting with reasoning-state noise (as in PTRM) against resampling the answer state.
- **Finding:** answer-state resampling recovers more failures at equal compute, and the gap widens with budget; restarting matches continuing, so retries parallelize.
- **Method (GAR):** at most 64 restarts, no training: 85.9 → 99.4 percent on Sudoku-Extreme and 68.8 → 85.6 percent on Pencil Puzzle Bench validation.
- **vs PTRM:** beats PTRM on the same checkpoints even when PTRM uses 42–197x the compute.
- **Caveat:** tested only on TRM.
- **Why added:** analyses where failures live in a recursive reasoner's two latent states.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, ICML 2026) is discussed in the introduction and related work (training with noise on both states, its initial-state ablation); not a baseline.

### LoopREG: Representation Supervision Enables Test-Time Recurrent Depth in Diffusion Transformers

[arXiv:2610.17877](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17877)

**TL;DR:** Looping the representation-supervised block of a diffusion Transformer, and adding visits at test time, cuts SiT-B/2 FID from 14.71 to 5.34.

- **Mechanism:** inside each denoiser call, the REG block aligned to frozen DINOv2 features is re-applied; alignment comes after the final training-time visit; extra visits at inference add no parameters.
- **Results:** SiT-B/2 at 400K steps: FID 15.08 (local REG) → 4.91. A model trained with two visits drops from 14.71 to 5.34 with a third visit (one extra block execution).
- **Scale:** gains persist on L/2 (4.23 → 3.21) and XL/2 (3.38 → 2.84).
- **Caveat:** plain looped SiT degrades with an extra visit (34.45 → 46.41); FID gains come with lower recall.
- **Why added:** the loop is a shared block inside one denoiser forward pass; diffusion is only the outer sampler.

### Monitoring latent reasoning in looped language models

[arXiv:2610.18127](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18127)

**TL;DR:** Across eight released looped LMs, most tokens are already decided at the first loop, and models that carry the prompt only in their state collapse under noise.

- **Setup:** five lenses (state settling, commitment iteration, per-iteration readout, attention-head roles, noise injection) on eight models from four families.
- **Two designs:** carry loops (Ouro, LoopUS) see the prompt only through the looped state; re-read loops (Huginn, Rec-OLMo, Parcae) re-inject it each iteration.
- **Findings:** in six of eight models, 76–89 percent of tokens settle on their final top-1 at iteration 1; Ouro-Thinking is best at iteration 1 and worsens with more loops; only Huginn improves steadily.
- **Robustness:** noise at 4x the entry-state norm breaks every carry model, while re-read models tolerate 50x; smaller noise drops Ouro-1.4B's refusal rate from 73 to 25 percent.
- **Why added:** a mechanistic and monitoring study of shared-block latent iteration.

### Looped Reasoning Finishes Earlier Than You Think

[arXiv:2610.18174](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18174) · Code: [camail-official/Quotient_Recurrent_Reasoner_QRR](https://github.com/camail-official/Quotient_Recurrent_Reasoner_QRR)

**TL;DR:** Slow oscillatory modes make recurrent reasoners converge late; QRR removes them by symmetry and halts at step 27 instead of 122 on Sudoku-Extreme, with higher accuracy.

- **Analysis:** a Sylvester decomposition of the local Jacobian shows a slowly decaying oscillatory mode imposes its rate on the coupled latent dynamics in FPRM and HRM; causal interventions confirm it.
- **Method (QRR):** a gauge-equivariant looped block with momentum that halts on a quotient-space fixed-point residual.
- **Results (7M parameters):** 97.9 Sudoku-Extreme, 85.8 Maze-Hard, 97.0 Maze-Unique, 62.4 Pass@1 Mini-ARC, vs 83.7, 85.3, 94.7, 57.1 for reproduced FPRM.
- **Halting:** median halt step 122 → 27; false-halt rate 8.9 → 0.1 percent.
- **Caveat:** each step costs more (34.7 vs 22.3 ms).
- **Why added:** explains and fixes slow convergence of a shared recurrent block.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, 2026) is a Table 1 baseline (89.9 Sudoku-Extreme, 88.9 Maze-Unique), Maze-Unique follows the EqR construction, and EqR appears in related work.

### Input-Conditioned Search for Efficient Test-Time Scaling of Recursive Reasoners

[arXiv:2610.18547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18547) · Code: [icsearch/ICS](https://github.com/icsearch/ICS)

**TL;DR:** Instead of looping longer or adding noise, rerun a frozen TRM under altered inputs, which can remove wrong attractors; Sudoku-Extreme goes from 87.85 to 99.81 percent.

- **Method:** inject tentative partial assignments or validity-preserving restatements into the input, then rerun the reasoner from a fresh state.
- **Theory:** computer-assisted proofs on trained Sudoku reasoners show an input change can remove an incorrect attracting fixed point through a fold bifurcation.
- **Results:** on Sudoku-Extreme, TRM with depth scaling scores 87.85 percent at 0.38 TFLOPs, ICS 99.81 percent at 1.12 TFLOPs; across six tasks it has the highest mean accuracy with up to two orders of magnitude fewer FLOPs than the scaling baselines.
- **Borderline:** each search branch is a full rerun of the reasoner; the catalog already files comparable TRM wrappers such as PTRM.
- **Why added:** test-time scaling and fixed-point analysis for a recursive reasoner.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, ICML 2026) is a baseline in the main comparison, run from the official code, and is cited for Maze-Unique being near-saturated.

### Tiny Recursive Message Passing

[arXiv:2610.18649](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18649)

**TL;DR:** The TRM training recipe applied to weight-shared message passing gives a 16k-parameter model with about 99 percent on Sudoku-Extreme that transfers to larger N-Queens and SAT instances.

- **Mechanism:** weight-shared GRU and Deep Sets modules run inner rounds while the answer state is fixed; a small GRU updates the answer each outer cycle; trained with deep supervision, truncated recursion, ACT halting and deduction lattices.
- **Results:** 16k parameters reach about 99 percent on Sudoku-Extreme in about 10 minutes on an H100; best-of-64 noisy passes reach 99.9 percent on Sudoku-Extreme and 100 percent on Maze-Unique.
- **Finding:** the cost of truncating gradients grows with how many rounds information needs to cross the graph, so wiring trades robustness to truncation against size transfer.
- **Transfer:** zero-shot to larger N-Queens boards and harder SAT formulas, matching or beating NeuroSAT and QuerySAT with a fraction of their parameters.
- **Why added:** a shared message-passing processor trained as a deep recursive reasoner.
- **Cites Benhao's work:** yes. It uses the maze-30x30-unique set released with EqR (locuslab/EqR) and cites EqR in related work.

## Near misses

- [Coarse-Graining Recursive Expert Computation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14413)
  replaces spans of untied GPT-2 and Qwen3 blocks with cheaper macros; its only shared
  recursion is a small toy prototype.
- [DeepThink](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16171) proposes a shared thinking operator over a
  knowledge bank, but it is purely conceptual, has no experiments, and parameter sharing is
  optional.
- [FON](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16301) uses content-adaptive recursion across sequence
  positions; its optional depth reuse is an ablation, not the contribution.
- [DEFlow](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.16597) solves an implicit Euler flow-policy sampler
  as an equilibrium system, which is flow-based iterative inference rather than a reused
  representation block.
- [Dynamic K-Head](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.17679) selects the number of CoDI continuous
  thought steps, which feed the last hidden state back through the full model as the next
  input.
- [LoOPSD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18162) runs a searched block of pretrained layers
  twice to build a self-distillation teacher, but the deployed student does not loop.
