# Daily Loop-Model Briefing - 2026-10-12

## Outcome

Ten papers were added. Each reapplies a shared learned block within one forward pass.
Four work on looped language models (post-training, safety, elastic depth, quantized
prefill), three improve how recursive reasoners read out or carry their state, and three
apply looped models to vision, RNA, and ARC. Two of the additions,
[Invariant-Measure Reasoners](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10996) and
[InfiLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11570), cite Benhao Huang's Equilibrium Reasoners
(EqR).

## Added

### Recurrent Self-Improvement: Dynamic Cross-Loop On-Policy Distillation for Looped Language Models

[LoopOPD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10623) post-trains Ouro looped language models
without an external teacher. A frozen loop-4 exit of the same weight-tied model teaches
the loop-2 exit on rollouts the student generates, which gives dense token-level
supervision. D-LoopOPD refreshes the teacher every step; because both exits share
parameters, improving the student also moves the teacher, and the authors give
sufficient conditions under which one update improves both depths. Trained only on
DAPO-Math-17k, Ouro-2.6B-Thinking goes from 55.49 (vanilla) and 56.60 (GRPO) to 62.36
average Avg@16 on AIME 2024/2025/2026, with AIME 2024 rising from 62.08 to 71.67. The
loop-2 exit improves from 39.16 to 47.50.

Why it was added: the teacher and student are depths of one shared recurrent block, and
the method exploits that tying.

### Safe at One Loop, Risky at Another: Aligning Safety Across Recurrent Depths in Looped Language Models

[This paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10625) attacks Ouro-1.4B and Ouro-2.6B at each
recurrent depth with GCG, AutoDAN, and PAIR on four safety benchmarks. Deeper recurrence
helps on plain harmful prompts, but jailbreak success can rise with depth (peaking at
depth 3 on Ouro-2.6B), and attacks built at one depth transfer to others. SFT, DPO,
SafeDPO, and naive all-depth DPO leave these gaps. SafeBridge adds depth-specific FiLM
modulation of the shared layers, a gated bridge that reuses an earlier, safer depth's
hidden state, and objectives that weight the weakest exits. Overall attack success falls
from 44.93 percent (DPO) to 9.85 percent on Ouro-2.6B and from 21.06 to 4.56 percent on
Ouro-1.4B, with comparable over-refusal.

Why it was added: it studies a failure mode specific to reading out a shared block at
different recurrent depths and fixes it in the loop.

### Invariant-Measure Reasoners: Stable Representations for Latent Reasoning

[ImR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.10996) notes that the latent states of recurrent-depth
reasoners often settle onto a compact set without reaching a fixed point, so a head read
from the last state can flip across depths. It instead predicts from the expected head
output under the invariant measure of the recurrent block, approximated by a running
average of head outputs over iterations, either by fine-tuning only the head or by
training from scratch with truncated BPTT. Trained from scratch on EqR, accuracy rises
from 89.4 to 98.8 percent on Maze-Unique 30x30 and from 96.2 to 98.7 percent on
Sudoku-Extreme. Head-only fine-tuning of a deep-thinking network improves out-of-
distribution mazes up to 99x99. Over 97 percent of ImR's non-fixed-point predictions are
stable and correct.

Why it was added: it changes how predictions are read from a reused block's long-run
dynamics.

**Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, *Equilibrium Reasoners*, ICML
2026) is the main base model, and the Maze-Unique data comes from EqR.

### From Chain-of-Thought to Loops: Non-Autoregressive Latent Reasoning via Looped Transformers

[LLoCoT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11472) replaces left-to-right latent chain-of-thought
with looped refinement of 64 learned latent slots. A Qwen3-1.7B backbone is reapplied
with tied weights (two loops by default), updating all slots jointly from the prompt and
the current workspace; a Gaussian head's mean is fed back between loops, and the final
head samples all latent thoughts in parallel to condition an autoregressive answer
decoder. On HumanEval, HumanEval+, MBPP, and MBPP+ it averages 63.6, against 63.2 for
reasoning SFT and 62.0 for NF-CoT, while cutting time to the first answer token from
3.17 s to 0.09 s. Analytical FLOPs are 1.6x higher, and accuracy is not monotonic in loop
count from 1 to 8.

Why it was added: the latent workspace is refined by repeatedly applying one weight-tied
transformer. It is a borderline case because each loop is a full backbone pass.

### Scaling to Tens of Thousands of Test-Time Iterations with Loop-Native Attention Residuals

[InfiLoop](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11570) argues that the carry-last state update of
looped transformers lets noisy updates overwrite solved deductions as iterations grow.
It replaces it with a learned attention residual over all past iterates, scored by a
pseudo-query with learned exponential decay, which runs as an exact O(d) streaming
recurrence and adds 513 parameters per loop axis to a 7M TRM-style reasoner. It reaches
97.9 percent on Sudoku-Extreme (TRM 74.7), 87.4 percent on Maze-Hard, and 49.1 and 13.6
percent pass@2 on ARC-AGI-1 and ARC-AGI-2. Extending test-time looping to 24,960 layer
evaluations keeps improving Sudoku accuracy, and the learned step size jumps to 0.91 when
a puzzle is solved. Code: [pixeli99/InfiLoop](https://github.com/pixeli99/InfiLoop).

Why it was added: it is a new state-carrying rule for a shared recursive block.

**Cites Benhao's work:** yes. EqR (Huang, Geng, Kolter, ICML 2026) is a baseline in the
main table (93.0 on Sudoku-Extreme) and is cited in related work on fixed-point latent
reasoning.

### One Block, Multiple Depths: Recurrent Vision Transformers with Depth-Programmed Experts

[reViT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12448) runs a ViT as one pre-norm Transformer block
applied at every depth. Attention and norms are shared; each depth's FFN is a
weight-space merge of a small shared expert bank, with merge weights produced by a router
that sees only normalized depth, so compute stays at one FFN per step. Trained from
scratch on ImageNet-1k with four experts, reViT-B/16 reaches 83.0 percent against DeiT
III's 82.8 with 23.6M instead of 86.6M parameters at similar FLOPs. Distilled from
DINOv2-B/14 using final features only, eight experts reach 83.9 percent on ImageNet and
45.8 mIoU on ADE20k. Weight merging beats token-dispatch and output-mixture MoE at the
same one-FFN budget.

Why it was added: a single block is reused across all depths of the encoder, with
depth-routed expert weights.

### One Model to Loop Them All: Elastic Latent Recurrence for Language Models

[ELR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.12582) removes the one-loop tax of looped language
models: a single pass costs and scores like a same-size non-recurrent model, and each
extra loop of the shared middle block buys accuracy. It uses a gated state update with a
learned step size, mixed loop counts of 1 to 4 within each optimizer update (introduced
only for the last 25 percent of tokens, about 1.16x training cost), and zero-initialized
conditioning on loop index and budget. On 4B and 30B-A3B hybrid models, one loop is 0.52
below and 1.04 above the matched baselines on a ten-task average, and four loops add 7.25
and 4.75 points. Last-loop KV caching keeps attention memory at one-loop size, and the
released Koi-4B moves from an estimated 18 to 29 on the AA-Index.

Why it was added: it trains a prelude-core-coda looped language model with elastic loop
count.

### RAMP: Recurrent Adaptive Mixed Precision for Efficient Prefill in Recurrent-Depth Language Models

[RAMP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13041) analyzes how weight-and-activation quantization
error propagates through Huginn-0125's 32 recurrent steps. Errors injected early are
damped by the steps that follow, and a bound shows that low precision belongs early
under a fixed budget. RAMP runs the first 24 steps with FP8 linears and the last 8 in
BF16, without retraining. At 4K context on WikiText, NLL rises by 0.00010 over BF16,
against 0.00516 for all-FP8 and 0.00444 for the reversed schedule, with 98.73 percent
top-1 agreement. Prefill on an H100 is up to 1.16x faster at 4K, but slower below 512
tokens.

Why it was added: it is a precision schedule over the iterations of a shared recurrent
block.

### Scaling RNA Foundation Models through Iterative Refinement

[Eco-RNA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13106) is an 80M-parameter bidirectional RNA model
built as prelude, shared input-conditioned Transformer core, and coda. The core updates a
latent state with a learned retention coefficient and input injection, trained with
masked reconstruction at sampled depths, truncated backpropagation, a fixed-point
displacement penalty, and a one-sided anchor to a shallower depth. On the 70-assay
RNAGym fitness benchmark, Spearman rises from 0.242 at one iteration to 0.286 at four,
while a parameter-matched fixed-depth control scores 0.224. At four iterations it beats
Evo 2 40B on all three aggregate fitness metrics with 500x fewer parameters.

Why it was added: it applies a recurrent-depth core to RNA modeling and shows depth
scaling at fixed parameters.

### Pretraining and Test-Time Training Budgets Matter for ARC: A Controlled Study of VARC and TRM

[This study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13660), accepted at BNAIC/BeNeLearn 2026, crosses
five pretraining budgets with four per-task test-time-training budgets for the recursive
TRM (7M) and the ViT-based VARC (18M) on ARC-AGI-1 under one inductive protocol. More
test-time sample presentations raise pass@2 in every setting (median gains 6.13 for VARC
and 5.38 for TRM). More test-time task variants help TRM in nine of ten comparisons but
VARC in only three. Pretraining improves all 16 TRM cells and lifts its best pass@2 from
19.63 to 37.50 percent, against 35.50 to 40.00 for VARC. Code:
[AdrienDeliege/arc-training-budgets](https://github.com/AdrienDeliege/arc-training-budgets).

Why it was added: it is an empirical analysis of what drives a recursive reasoner's ARC
results.

## Near misses

- [GraphPDHG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07255) aligns message passing with
  primal-dual hybrid gradient steps, but its layers are separately parameterized, so no
  processor is reused.
- [Stepped MoE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.07348) nests elastic MoE subnetworks for
  different deployment sizes; no block repeats within a forward pass.
- [Spatial Latent Reasoning](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.09418) generates Coconut-style
  continuous thoughts from an untied multimodal LLM, which is autoregressive latent
  reasoning.
- [Lapras](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.11111) feeds a time-series LLM's last hidden state
  back as the next input embedding across full forward passes, not a reused internal
  block.
